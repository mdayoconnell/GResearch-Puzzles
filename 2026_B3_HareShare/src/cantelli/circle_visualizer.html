Why Islands Beat the Block at t = 2, 3

For small jump times \(t \in \{2, 3\}\), placing a single stump on an island equidistant from both shores of a length-\((q-1)\) block sacrifices 1 survivor in the unit direction \(a=1\), but creates new cross-circle arithmetic progressions in multiple other directions \(a\) that bridge the island with both shores simultaneously.

Why the Block Dominates in the Tail (t ≥ ⌈q/2⌉)

When \(t \ge \lceil q/2 \rceil\), two disjoint runs of length \(\ge t+1\) would require \(2(t+1) > q\) total stumps, which is impossible. Thus every direction \(a\) has at most one surviving run. The block maximizes this single run in direction \(a=1\), giving the exact tail formula \(F_{\max}(p,t) = 3q - 2t\).
